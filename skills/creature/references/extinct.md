# Extinct animals: evidence by trait

An extinct animal needs a specific taxon, chosen reconstruction basis, relevant specimens/publications, and a time-stamped evidence review. Do not apply a blanket "unknown" label to everything or present every design decision as equally certain.

## Record support per trait

| Evidence status | Meaning | Example of appropriate wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `observed` | Directly preserved or documented evidence relevant to the selected specimen/taxon. | "Surface impression preserved in specimen X; extent outside that patch is unresolved." |
| `inferred` | Reconstruction using anatomy, relatives, biomechanics, or other comparisons. | "Muscle volume inferred from attachment areas and selected comparative anatomy." |
| `disputed` | Credible sources support incompatible interpretations. | "This model uses interpretation A; B remains an alternative." |
| `artistic` | A choice that available evidence does not resolve. | "Eye color is a design choice." |

For every claim, identify the actual trait and cited support. Keep taxon, specimen, location on the body, and limits of inference distinct. Evidence from one close relative does not automatically become direct evidence for this animal.

Reconstruct skeletal landmarks first, then soft tissue volume, coverings, color, and movement. Preserve alternatives in interpretation notes; do not silently combine mutually exclusive reconstructions. Evidence should be revisited when publications change.

The Natural History Museum describes several distinct sources of reconstruction evidence, including skeletons, tracks, skin impressions, preserved soft tissues, and comparisons with living animals. These sources support different kinds of claims. Use its overview to plan the research, then seek the relevant specimen records and primary papers for the selected taxon. [NHM: what we know about dinosaurs and how](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/what-can-scientists-learn-about-dinosaurs-and-how.html) (accessed 2026-10-18).

## Capability boundary

The initial runtime has no extinct species profile. Category/evidence validation does not create a suitable mesh or rig. Research and draft the specification, then use the extension workflow for the required body and behavior. Do not repurpose a horse or cow with a new name and call it a reviewed reconstruction.

Render disputed or artistic traits clearly in the accompanying notes. An optional explanatory panel can link traits to their evidence; do not obscure the creature with permanent disclaimer labels.

Block release when anatomy contradicts strong chosen evidence, inference is presented as observation, or visible features lack the declared reconstruction rationale. An appealing result may still be an explicitly identified artistic interpretation.
