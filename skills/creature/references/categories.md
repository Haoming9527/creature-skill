# Category routing

Category answers **what makes the portrayal correct**. Body module answers **what geometry and rig can build it**. Species/design profile answers **which proportions and defining features to use**. Keep those choices independent.

| Public category | Evidence contract | Blocking errors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `living` | Match the selected species, breed/type, life stage, sex, condition, anatomy, and supported movement against credible observation. | Wrong identity/anatomy, unsupported trait described as normal, incorrect gait, or an unreviewed proportion change presented as anatomically approved. |
| `extinct` | Distinguish observed evidence, inferred reconstruction, disputed interpretation, and artistic choices for relevant traits. | Contradicting strong evidence, certainty without support, or mixing incompatible reconstructions without explanation. |
| `fictional` | Match the brief and chosen source/portrayal; preserve coherent form and declared movement rules. | Missing defining features, unexplained source departures, incoherent articulation, or representing story/report evidence as proof of existence. |

All categories require good surfaces, stable deformation, meaningful controls, complete local assets, portable export, and honest performance results. Fictional permits extraordinary anatomy; it does not excuse broken meshes or arbitrary behavior.

## Routing examples

- Adult Holstein cow: `living`, ungulate quadruped, Holstein profile.
- Documented extinct species: `extinct`; choose a body module only after examining its anatomy and evidence. No extinct profile is validated in v1.
- An invented equine creature, or a portrayal from a legend: `fictional`; record which details come from the user's brief or chosen source. Only features implemented by its module can be generated.
- A formerly fictional name that also identifies a real species: clarify only if the requested appearance and context do not settle the intended identity.

Do not add public subcategories. A source-based portrayal and an original design use the same fictional category; their design notes differ.

## Conflicting follow-up edits

If a living animal is asked to have an impossible or unsupported trait, keep the source specification intact. Explain the specific conflict and ask whether to retain realistic anatomy or make a fictional derivative. On explicit agreement, create a new specification with lineage notes and rerun the appropriate rules. Do not silently change the category to bypass validation.

Distinguish an unsupported capability from an impossible biological trait. A realistic bird is possible but needs a bird module. State the missing module and extension work rather than claiming the animal itself violates realism.
